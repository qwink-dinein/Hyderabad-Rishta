## 1. Character and conduct

Ask people who know the person — teachers, colleagues, neighbours, the local masjid community. How do they treat parents, siblings and people who work for them? Character usually shows in small, everyday behaviour.

## 2. Practice and values

Talk openly about prayer, fasting, family values and expectations. The goal is not a particular answer — it is that both sides understand each other and are comfortable.

## 3. Education, work and stability

Is the person settled in work or study, and do their plans fit your family’s expectations — city, travel, career after marriage? Confirm the details the biodata gives.

## 4. Family and living arrangements

Joint or nuclear family, where the couple will live, responsibilities toward parents. These questions cause more friction after marriage than almost anything else, so ask them early.

## 5. Compatibility between the two people

Age gap, temperament, interests, education level and how each wants to spend their life. Give the two people a chance to talk — with family involved — before any decision.

## 6. Money and expectations

Discuss mehr, wedding expectations and finances respectfully and early. Any demand that makes you uncomfortable is worth pausing over.

## Red flags

- Pressure to decide quickly or to keep things secret from family.
- Requests for money, gifts or documents early on.
- Details that change between conversations.
- Reluctance to let families meet.
