## Living arrangements

- Will the couple live with the groom’s family, separately, or plan to move later?
- Which city? Is either side open to relocating, including abroad?

## Work and study

- Will both continue working or studying after marriage?
- Any plans for further education, a job change or moving for work?

## Practice and values

- How does each person practise, and what do they hope for in their home?
- How will the families celebrate, and what traditions matter to each side?

## Money

- Mehr, wedding expenses and who covers what.
- Responsibilities toward parents or siblings.
- Any debts or major commitments.

## Children and family life

- Hopes about children and when.
- Roles and responsibilities at home.

## Health and history

- Previous marriages, children from a previous marriage.
- Anything important the other family should know, shared respectfully.

## How to have these conversations

Let elders talk to elders first, then give the two people time to speak — with family involved. Ask openly, listen without judgement, and write down what you agree on. For proposal red flags, see [what families should look for](/blog/what-families-should-look-for-in-a-marriage-proposal/).
