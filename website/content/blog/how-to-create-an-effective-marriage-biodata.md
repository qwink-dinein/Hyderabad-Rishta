## Lead with the facts families check first

Date of birth, height, education, profession, city and marital status. If these are clear, families keep reading.

## Write an "about" that sounds like a person

Bad: "Simple, caring, family-oriented." Better: "Works as a teacher in Mehdipatnam, loves reading and cooking for the family, and hopes to keep teaching after marriage." Three or four specific sentences beat a paragraph of adjectives.

## Be honest about practice

Describe prayer, fasting and Qur’an reading as they are. Families value honesty, and it saves everyone time.

## Choose the photo carefully

One recent, clear, simple photo — face visible, good light, no group photos or heavy filters. Decide who can see it.

## Keep preferences reasonable

List what truly matters. "Educated, practising, from a respectful family, Hyderabad preferred" is better than ten strict conditions.

## Complete the family section

Parents’ occupations, siblings and how many are married, family type and where the family lives — without names or phone numbers.

## Update it

Change the biodata when anything changes — a new job, a move, a finished degree. Out-of-date details cost trust.
